Law of Large Numbers

Author

John Robin Inston

Published

September 25, 2026

1 Overview

The weak and strong laws of large numbers are fundamental results in probability theory. Writing \(S_n=X_1+\dots+X_n\), they describe the long-run behaviour of the sample mean \[ \bar{X}_n=\frac{S_{n}}{n}=\frac{{X_{1}+\dots+X_{n}}}{n}. \] The weak law (WLLN) states that \(\bar{X}_n\) converges in probability to the expected value, and the strong law (SLLN) that it converges almost surely. The weak laws are proved with the Chebyshev Inequality and the strong law additionally uses the Borel-Cantelli Lemma. There are many versions with different strength of assumptions.

2 Weak Laws

The simplest result is for i.i.d. random variables with finite variance.

Let \(X_{1}, \dots, X_{n}\) be i.i.d. with finite mean \(\mathbb{E}[X_{i}]=\mu\) and finite variance \(\operatorname{Var}(X_{i})=\sigma^2<\infty\). Then the sample mean converges in probability and in \(L_2\) to \(\mu\), \[ \frac{S_{n}}{n} \stackrel{L_{2}~\&~\mathbb{P}}{\to}\mu\quad (n\to \infty), \] that is, for every \(\epsilon>0\), \(\lim_{ n \to \infty }\mathbb{P}(|\bar{X}_{n}-\mu|<\epsilon)=1\).

Proof. First, \(\mathbb{E}\left[ S_{n}/n \right]= \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n\mu=\mu\) and \[ \operatorname{Var}\left( \frac{S_{n}}{n} \right)= \frac{1}{n^2}\sum_{i=1}^n\operatorname{Var}(X_{i})= \frac{n\sigma^2}{n^2}=\frac{\sigma^2}{n}. \] Fixing \(\epsilon>0\), the Chebyshev Inequality gives \[ \mathbb{P}\left( \left| \frac{S_{n}}{n} -\mu\right| \geq \epsilon \right)\leq \frac{\sigma^2}{n\epsilon ^2}\to 0\quad(n\to\infty). \] Since \(\mathbb{E}|S_n/n-\mu|^2=\sigma^2/n\to0\) we also have \(L_2\) convergence.

The assumptions can be weakened to uncorrelated random variables with bounded variance.

Let \(X_{1}, X_{2}, \dots\) be uncorrelated random variables with \(\mathbb{E}[X_{i}]=\mu\) and \(\operatorname{Var}(X_{i})\leq C<\infty\). Then \[ \frac{S_{n}}{n} \stackrel{L_{2}~\&~\mathbb{P}}{\to}\mu\quad (n\to \infty). \]

Proof. By uncorrelatedness the variances add, so \[ \mathbb{E}\left\lvert \frac{S_{n}}{n}-\mu \right\rvert ^2=\operatorname{Var}\left( \frac{S_{n}}{n} \right)=n^{-2}\sum_{i=1}^n \operatorname{Var}(X_{i})\leq n^{-2}\cdot n\cdot C \to 0. \] Convergence in probability follows from the Chebyshev Inequality (or from the fact that \(L^2\) convergence implies convergence in probability, see Convergence in Lp Norm).

The most general version gives conditions under which the sample mean, centred by a suitable truncated mean, converges in probability.

Let \(\{ X_{n} \}\) be independent random variables such that

  1. \(\sum_{j=1}^n \mathbb{P}(\lvert X_{j} \rvert>n)\to 0\) as \(n \to \infty\); and
  2. \(\frac{1}{n^2}\sum_{j=1}^n\mathbb{E}[X_{j}^2 \mathbb{1}_{\lvert X_{j} \rvert \leq n}]\to 0\) as \(n \to \infty\).

Define \(a_{n}:=\sum_{j=1}^n \mathbb{E}[X_{j}\mathbb{1}_{\lvert X_{j} \rvert\leq n}]\). Then \(\frac{{S_n-a_n}}{n}\stackrel{\mathbb{P}}{\to}0\) as \(n \to \infty\).

Proof. Truncate: let \(T_n=\sum_{j=1}^nX_j\mathbb{1}_{\lvert X_{j} \rvert\leq n}\), so \(\mathbb{E}T_n=a_n\). Then \(\mathbb{P}(S_n\neq T_n)\leq\sum_{j=1}^n\mathbb{P}(\lvert X_j\rvert>n)\to0\) by condition 1. By independence and condition 2, \[ \operatorname{Var}\left(\frac{T_n}{n}\right)\leq\frac{1}{n^2}\sum_{j=1}^n\mathbb{E}[X_{j}^2 \mathbb{1}_{\lvert X_{j} \rvert \leq n}]\to0, \] so the Chebyshev Inequality gives \(\mathbb{P}(|T_n-a_n|>\epsilon n)\to0\) for all \(\epsilon>0\). Finally \[ \mathbb{P}\left(\frac{|S_n-a_n|}{n}>\epsilon\right)\leq\mathbb{P}(S_n\neq T_n)+\mathbb{P}(|T_n-a_n|>\epsilon n)\to0. \]

Two classical versions follow from the general law. Feller’s WLLN drops the often restrictive first moment assumption.

Let \(\{ X_{n} \}\) be i.i.d. with \(\lim_{x \to \infty}x\mathbb{P}(\lvert X_{1} \rvert>x)=0\). Then \[ \frac{S_{n}}{n}-\mathbb{E}[X_{1}\mathbb{1}_{\lvert X_{1} \rvert \leq n}]\stackrel{\mathbb{P}}{\to}0\quad \text{as}\quad n \to \infty. \]

Proof. Condition 1 of the general law reads \(n\mathbb{P}(|X_1|>n)\to0\), which is the assumption. For condition 2 we need \(\frac1n\mathbb{E}[X_1^2\mathbb{1}_{|X_1|\leq n}]\to0\). Writing \(\mathbb{E}[X_1^2\mathbb{1}_{|X_1|\leq n}]\leq\int_0^n2x\,\mathbb{P}(|X_1|>x)\,dx\) and using that \(2x\mathbb{P}(|X_1|>x)\to0\), the Cesàro average \(\frac1n\int_0^n2x\,\mathbb{P}(|X_1|>x)\,dx\to0\). Finally \(a_n/n=\mathbb{E}[X_1\mathbb{1}_{|X_1|\leq n}]\).

Khinchine’s WLLN is the version for i.i.d. sequences with a finite first moment.

Let \(\{ X_{n} \}\) be i.i.d. with \(\mathbb{E}[\lvert X_{1} \rvert]< \infty\) and \(\mathbb{E}[X_{1}]=\mu\). Then \[ \frac{S_{n}}{n}\stackrel{\mathbb{P}}{\to}\mu\quad\text{as}\quad n \to \infty. \]

Proof. We check the conditions of the general law. Condition 1 holds since \(n\mathbb{P}(|X_1|>n)\leq\mathbb{E}[|X_1|\mathbb{1}_{|X_1|>n}]\to0\). For condition 2 and any \(\epsilon>0\), \[ \frac1n\mathbb{E}[X_1^2\mathbb{1}_{|X_1|\leq n}]\leq\epsilon\,\mathbb{E}|X_1|+\mathbb{E}[|X_1|\mathbb{1}_{|X_1|>\epsilon n}]\to\epsilon\,\mathbb{E}|X_1|, \] and \(\epsilon\) is arbitrary. Finally \(a_n/n=\mathbb{E}[X_1\mathbb{1}_{|X_1|\leq n}]\to\mu\) by the Dominated Convergence Theorem.

3 Strong Laws

The Strong Law of Large Numbers (SLLN) is deeper than the weak law: the sample average converges almost surely (with probability 1) to the expected value. We start with the version requiring finite second moments.

Let \(X_{1}, X_{2},\dots\) be i.i.d. with \(\mathbb{E}[X_{i}]=\mu\) and \(\mathbb{E}[X_{i}^2]<\infty\). Then \[ \bar{X}_{n}\stackrel{a.s.}{\to}\mu\quad (n \to \infty), \] that is, \(\mathbb{P}(\lim_{ n \to \infty }\bar{X}_{n}=\mu)=1\).

Proof. Replacing \(X_i\) by \(X_i-\mu\) we may assume \(\mu=0\); let \(\sigma^2=\operatorname{Var}(X_1)\).

Step 1: squares. By the Chebyshev Inequality, \(\mathbb{P}(|S_{n^2}|>\epsilon n^2)\leq\sigma^2/(\epsilon^2n^2)\), which is summable in \(n\). By the Borel-Cantelli Lemma, \(S_{n^2}/n^2\to0\) a.s.

Step 2: in between. Let \(D_n=\max_{n^2\leq k<(n+1)^2}|S_k-S_{n^2}|\). Bounding the maximum by a sum over the \(2n+1\) indices, each term having second moment at most \(2n\sigma^2\), \(\mathbb{E}D_n^2\leq(2n+1)2n\sigma^2\leq 6n^2\sigma^2\). Hence \(\mathbb{P}(D_n>\epsilon n^2)\leq6\sigma^2/(\epsilon^2n^2)\) is summable and \(D_n/n^2\to0\) a.s. by Borel-Cantelli.

For \(n^2\leq k<(n+1)^2\) we then have \(|S_k|/k\leq(|S_{n^2}|+D_n)/n^2\to0\) a.s.

Finally, the general version only needs pairwise independence and a finite first moment.

Let \(X_{1}, X_{2}, \dots\) be pairwise independent identically distributed random variables with \(\mathbb{E}\lvert X_{1} \rvert<\infty\) and \(\mathbb{E}[X_{1}]=\mu\). Then \[ \frac{S_{n}}{n}= \frac{\sum_{i=1}^nX_{i}}{n} \stackrel{a.s.}{\to}\mu\quad \text{as}\quad n \to \infty. \]

The proof combines truncation, the subsequence argument above with geometrically growing indices, and the Borel-Cantelli lemma; see Etemadi (1981) or Durrett.
