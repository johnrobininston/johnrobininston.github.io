Images and Preimages of Functions

Images and preimages of subsets under a function: definitions, how each interacts with set operations, and the relation between them.
Author

John Robin Inston

Published

October 30, 2026

1 Images and Preimages

A function \(f:X\to Y\) acts on points, but it also acts on subsets. Pushing a subset of \(X\) forward gives its image, and pulling a subset of \(Y\) back gives its preimage. Preimages interact very well with set operations, which is why they are the right tool in Measure Theory: a Measurable Function is defined by the condition that preimages of measurable sets are measurable.

2 Definitions

Let \(f:X\to Y\) be a function.

The image of a subset \(A\subseteq X\) is

\[ f(A) = \{f(x) : x\in A\} = \{y\in Y : \exists\,x\in A,\ f(x)=y\}. \]

The preimage (or inverse image) of a subset \(B\subseteq Y\) is

\[ f^{-1}(B) = \{x\in X : f(x)\in B\}. \]

The notation \(f^{-1}(B)\) does not require \(f\) to be invertible. Both operations are maps between power sets,

\[ f:\mathcal{P}(X)\to\mathcal{P}(Y), \qquad f^{-1}:\mathcal{P}(Y)\to\mathcal{P}(X), \]

and both are defined for every function. The range of \(f\) is the image \(f(X)\), which can be strictly smaller than the codomain \(Y\).

Let \(f(x)=x^2\) on \(\mathbb{R}\). Then \(f(\mathbb{R})=[0,\infty)\) and \(f([-2,1])=[0,4]\). For preimages, \(f^{-1}([1,4])=[-2,-1]\cup[1,2]\), \(f^{-1}(\{0\})=\{0\}\) and \(f^{-1}((-\infty,0))=\emptyset\).

3 Properties of the Preimage

The preimage commutes with all set operations. For subsets \(B,B_1,B_2\subseteq Y\) and a family \((B_i)_{i\in I}\) indexed by an arbitrary set \(I\):

Operation Identity
Unions \(f^{-1}\left(\bigcup_i B_i\right)=\bigcup_i f^{-1}(B_i)\)
Intersections \(f^{-1}\left(\bigcap_i B_i\right)=\bigcap_i f^{-1}(B_i)\)
Complements \(f^{-1}(Y\setminus B)=X\setminus f^{-1}(B)\)
Differences \(f^{-1}(B_1\setminus B_2)=f^{-1}(B_1)\setminus f^{-1}(B_2)\)
Monotonicity \(B_1\subseteq B_2\implies f^{-1}(B_1)\subseteq f^{-1}(B_2)\)
Composition \((g\circ f)^{-1}(C)=f^{-1}\left(g^{-1}(C)\right)\)

Proof. The statement \(x\in f^{-1}(B)\) means only that \(f(x)\in B\), which is a pointwise membership test, so it passes through any logical operation. For example,

\[ x\in f^{-1}(B_1\cap B_2) \iff f(x)\in B_1 \text{ and } f(x)\in B_2 \iff x\in f^{-1}(B_1)\cap f^{-1}(B_2), \]

and the same argument works for unions, complements and differences.

This is why preimages are used in measure theory. If \(\mathcal{G}\) is a \(\sigma\)-algebra on \(Y\), then \(\{f^{-1}(B):B\in\mathcal{G}\}\) is a \(\sigma\)-algebra on \(X\), and this is why a random variable is defined by the condition \(X^{-1}(B)\in\mathcal{F}\) (see Random Variables and Distribution Functions and Generated Sigma Algebra).

4 Properties of the Image

The image only partly commutes with set operations. For subsets \(A,A_1,A_2\subseteq X\) and a family \((A_i)_{i\in I}\):

Operation Relation
Unions \(f\left(\bigcup_i A_i\right)=\bigcup_i f(A_i)\)
Intersections \(f\left(\bigcap_i A_i\right)\subseteq\bigcap_i f(A_i)\)
Differences \(f(A_1)\setminus f(A_2)\subseteq f(A_1\setminus A_2)\)
Complements \(f(X)\setminus f(A)\subseteq f(X\setminus A)\)
Monotonicity \(A_1\subseteq A_2\implies f(A_1)\subseteq f(A_2)\)
Composition \((g\circ f)(A)=g\left(f(A)\right)\)

There is no general relation of the form \(f(X\setminus A)=Y\setminus f(A)\). One has \(Y\setminus f(A)\subseteq f(X\setminus A)\) when \(f\) is surjective, and equality when \(f\) is bijective.

Why intersections fail. Take \(f(x)=x^2\) on \(\mathbb{R}\), \(A_1=\{-1\}\) and \(A_2=\{1\}\). Then \(A_1\cap A_2=\emptyset\), so \(f(A_1\cap A_2)=\emptyset\), but \(f(A_1)\cap f(A_2)=\{1\}\). Two different points can collide in the image.

Equality is recovered exactly when \(f\) is injective.

The following are equivalent:

  1. \(f\) is injective;
  2. \(f(A_1\cap A_2)=f(A_1)\cap f(A_2)\) for all \(A_1,A_2\subseteq X\);
  3. \(f(A_1\setminus A_2)=f(A_1)\setminus f(A_2)\) for all \(A_1,A_2\subseteq X\).

Proof. The inclusion \(\subseteq\) in (2) and the inclusion \(\supseteq\) in (3) hold for every function. Suppose \(f\) is injective. If \(y\in f(A_1)\cap f(A_2)\), then \(y=f(a_1)=f(a_2)\) with \(a_i\in A_i\), and injectivity gives \(a_1=a_2\in A_1\cap A_2\), which proves (2). If \(y=f(a)\) with \(a\in A_1\setminus A_2\) and also \(y\in f(A_2)\), then \(y=f(a')\) with \(a'\in A_2\) and \(a=a'\), a contradiction, so \(y\notin f(A_2)\) and (3) holds.

Conversely, suppose \(f\) is not injective, so \(f(x_1)=f(x_2)\) for some \(x_1\neq x_2\). With \(A_1=\{x_1\}\) and \(A_2=\{x_2\}\), we have \(f(A_1\cap A_2)=\emptyset\ne f(A_1)\cap f(A_2)\), so (2) fails, and \(f(A_1\setminus A_2)=f(A_1)\ne\emptyset=f(A_1)\setminus f(A_2)\), so (3) fails.

5 Interaction of Image and Preimage

For \(A\subseteq X\) and \(B\subseteq Y\),

\[ A\subseteq f^{-1}\big(f(A)\big), \qquad f\big(f^{-1}(B)\big)=B\cap f(X)\subseteq B. \]

Moreover,

  • \(f^{-1}(f(A))=A\) for all \(A\subseteq X\) if and only if \(f\) is injective,
  • \(f(f^{-1}(B))=B\) for all \(B\subseteq Y\) if and only if \(f\) is surjective.

So injectivity and surjectivity are exactly the conditions under which \(f\) and \(f^{-1}\), as maps between power sets, invert each other.

The pair also forms a Galois connection,

\[ f(A)\subseteq B \iff A\subseteq f^{-1}(B). \]

This single equivalence encodes the two inclusions above: it means that \(f\) is the left adjoint of \(f^{-1}\) on the power-set lattices. As a right adjoint, the preimage preserves all intersections (limits), while the image, as a left adjoint, preserves all unions (colimits).

Two more useful identities are

\[ f\big(A\cap f^{-1}(B)\big)=f(A)\cap B, \qquad f^{-1}(B)=\emptyset \iff B\cap f(X)=\emptyset. \]

6 Summary

Operation Preimage \(f^{-1}\) Image \(f\)
Unions \(=\) \(=\)
Intersections \(=\) \(\subseteq\) (\(=\) iff injective)
Complements \(=\) (complement in the matching space) no general equality
Differences \(=\) \(f(A_1)\setminus f(A_2)\subseteq f(A_1\setminus A_2)\) (\(=\) iff injective)

Preimages are well behaved with respect to all set operations, while images are well behaved only with respect to unions.
