## Introduction

I recently watched the following YouTube video by PsychToons titled
[Psychology of Polymaths: Why Having Multiple Interests Is an Unfair Advantage](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D0SGbomVuKc&list=PLHQN-ld89Ctc&index=3&pp=iAQBsAgC) and it got me thinking
about what it means to have diverse interests and how one can properly leverage
a curious mind. We start by summarizing the definition of a polymath provided
on Wikipedia.

:::{.definition data-title="Polymath"}

A [polymath]{.accent} is an individual whose knowledge spans many different
subjects, known to draw on complex bodies of knowledge to solve specific
problems.

:::

Notable examples of the polymath architype include
include Sir Christopher Wren (architect, astronomer, mathematician and
physicist), Leonardo Davinci (painter, draughtsman, engineer, scientist,
sculptor) and Benjamin Franklin (writer, philosopher, politician, scientist).
Each individual was highly successful in multiple fields spanning both the
sciences and the arts.

## Lacking Focus

The video starts by outlining an all too real experience for people with
broad and changing interests: late nights reading into topics unrelated to
their work, unfinished projects, unfocussed application, and a resounding
feeling of frustration at ones own lack of focus or progress. Modern society
seems increasingly oriented towards hyper-specialization, whether it be
starting training for atheletic greatness at a young age, selecting your
chosen career specialization in high school, or playing instruments at
increasingly younger ages. Wherever you look you seem to see somebody
younger and more driven achieving your goals sooner than you, and this
game of comparing and constrasting serves only as a constant drain on
motivation. Why can't I have started training sooner? Why didn't I learn to
play the piano at 4 years old. I wish I could have earned a Ph.D. at the ripe
old age of 13 (Dr Karl Witte achieved this rather astonishing feat in 1814).

The folly of comparison and it's inevitable path to envy and jealousy
